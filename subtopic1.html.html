The Development of Gene Editing Technologies



URL The evolution and history of gene editing technologies
Site Author Shubhchintan Randhawa, Shatakshi Sengar
Abstract This site describes the history, mechanism, and application of the three main editing technologies, which are: zinc-finger nucleases, transcription activator-like effector nucleases, and CRISPR-Cas9. The website describes the procedure and timeline of Griffith's experiment by Frederick Griffith, which helped in understanding bacterial transformation. His findings were used as a framework to help scientists determine the makeup of genes. The article then describes how different scientists used Griffith’s findings to conduct alterations of the DNA sequence, which laid the foundation for future gene editing technologies. The site details Homology-Directed Repair (HDR) and Non-Homologous End Joining (NHEJ), which are two DNA repair mechanisms when there is a double-strand break in the DNA. It compares the DNA repair mechanisms to each other and discusses the pros and cons of both.




URL Genome Editing: Past, Present, and Future
Site Author Dana Carroll
Abstract The site includes a history of genome-editing methods and how they evolved. It discusses how genome editing experiments were conducted, who they were conducted on, and the limitations that were present. The site highlights three of the most powerful types of gene editing: ZFNs (zinc-finger nucleases), TALENs (transcription activator-like effector nucleases), and CRISPR-Cas9. It describes the techniques used by each of these gene-editing methods and their issues. The website also discusses the uses of different kinds of gene editing, such as in agriculture, medicine, and gene drives. The site discusses questions that arise about the consequences of gene editing, the safety of gene editing, and ethical issues of genetically changing someone's DNA.
