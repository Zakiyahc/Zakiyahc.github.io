 The Ethics of Germline Gene Editing - PMC
Site Author
 Christopher Gyngell, Thomas Douglas, Julian Savulescu
Abstract
 This site discusses the ethical arguments that support and oppose germline gene editing. It mentions the opinions of different groups, such as those who argue that human gene editing is morally wrong and other groups who propose a temporary ban on the clinical use of germline gene editing. The site provides an argument that germline gene editing can eliminate the passing on of single gene disorders. The site also highlights how germline gene editing can limit or reduce the development of polygenic diseases. It also discusses how germline gene editing has consequences such as unintended changes to the germline, lack of consent of future generations, and human enhancement.

 What are the Ethical Concerns of Genome Editing?
Site Author
 National Human Genome Research Institute
Abstract
 This site discusses the ethical considerations that must be made when it comes to gene editing. It describes different ethical considerations, such as safety when it comes to editing the wrong place or possible mosaicism. The site also mentions the lack of consent regarding genome editing in embryos, which will impact future generations. It also addresses the accessibility of gene editing, as many are concerned that only certain groups of people will have access to it. It highlights certain scenarios where genome editing can be a recommended choice, such as couples who are homozygous for a disease-carrying gene. The site also details the efforts that have been made regarding human genome editing in embryos.
